# The 500-Hour Plan: Research Synthesis & Architecture Brainstorm

This is the synthesis of nine deep-research streams (see `docs/research/`) plus an
adversarial critique pass. It is deliberately **not** the lesson plan — it's the
evidence base, the design decisions the evidence supports, the honest unknowns,
and the open questions to settle before writing the plan itself.

---

## 1. Where you're actually starting from

- A 4 on AP Spanish Language maps (per ACTFL's own — admittedly informal — guidance)
  to roughly **ACTFL Intermediate High ≈ CEFR B1+** in speaking at age ~18.
- The attrition literature is unusually kind to your profile. Bahrick's 50-year study
  of school-learned Spanish shows loss is front-loaded (years 1–6), then knowledge
  stabilizes in "**permastore**" for decades. Retention is predicted by depth of
  original training — an AP-4 learner sits near the top of that curve. Recognition
  and receptive skills survive far better than recall and production, which predicts
  exactly your current shape: can "get by" in Mexico, rusty at spontaneous speech.
- The **savings paradigm** (de Bot; Hansen's studies of returned missionaries) shows
  even apparently-forgotten material relearns dramatically faster than new material.
- Best guess pending measurement: speaking floor around **Intermediate Low–Mid**,
  listening/reading near **Intermediate High/B1+**. This gets *measured*, not
  assumed, in week 1 (see §6.5).

**Implication:** Phase 1 is *reactivation through production*, not re-instruction.
Treating yourself as a beginner would waste your single biggest asset. (Honest
caveat from the critique pass: savings evidence is mostly lexical; how fast
productive *syntax* recovers is under-studied. The reactivation phase is an
informed bet, not a proven protocol.)

## 2. What the evidence actually supports

### From the SLA literature (contested where noted)

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| Instruction beats naturalistic exposure (d ≈ 1.0 meta-analytic) | Robust, but measured on narrow treatment-aligned tests |
| Conversational interaction with negotiation of meaning drives acquisition; effects *grow* on delayed posttests | Among the most robust findings in the field |
| Oral corrective feedback works (d ≈ 0.64); **prompts that force self-repair (d ≈ 0.83) beat recasts (d ≈ 0.53)** | Robust; recasts especially weak in audio-only channels where they go unnoticed |
| Automatization is **skill-specific**: comprehension practice barely transfers to production (DeKeyser) | Experimentally supported; the core argument for a speaking-centered plan |
| Explicit grammar instruction helps | Real but **overstated** — much of the measured advantage is an artifact of tests that reward explicit knowledge. Do it, but evaluate it in free speech, not on drills |
| Fluency is trainable via task repetition under time pressure (4/3/2); gains proceduralize and transfer to new topics | Solid (de Jong & Perfetti), but accuracy needs separate attention |
| Nation's Four Strands (~25% each: meaning-focused input / output / deliberate study / fluency work) | Best available curriculum architecture; the 25% split is a principled heuristic, not a measured optimum |
| Input is necessary but not sufficient (Krashen's strong version is rejected; Swain's immersion evidence: thousands of input hours ≠ accurate production) | Consensus |

### From FSI / DLI / FBI

- FSI takes selected, full-time learners from zero to **ILR 3** (≈ ACTFL Superior)
  in Spanish in ~600–750 class hours **plus ~300–450 hours of homework** — call it
  ~1,000–1,100 total. Your 500 hours, starting from a dormant B1+, is *tight but
  credible* for a lower target (§4).
- FSI's own "lessons learned": time-on-task and intensity are *crucial* (shortened
  programs "have not worked"); explicit early grammar plus focus-on-form beats
  waiting for structures to "emerge"; adults can absolutely do this (FSI mean age 41);
  **native accent is a non-goal — intelligibility is the criterion**; free-wheeling
  multiparty conversation is the hardest skill and one-on-one practice under-trains it.
- The famous 1960s "FSI method" tapes are audio-lingual relics; FSI itself abandoned
  that. What survived: occasional *communicative* drills (answers that require
  processing meaning) for automatizing high-frequency patterns.
- High-stakes external testing (career consequences) is a deliberate motivation
  engine — the one pillar a solo learner must buy separately (§6.5).
- The FBI mostly *hires* speakers and tests them (ILR battery); it is not a
  training model worth copying. The trainable models are FSI/DLI.
- FSI's "critical mass" observation: skills parked below solid ILR 3 decay;
  at/above it they self-maintain. Plan the endgame accordingly (§4).

### From the Mormon MTC

The MTC is a 6-week, ~350–450-contact-hour *on-ramp*, not a fluency machine —
missionaries leave it task-functional and reach ~ACTFL Advanced (B2-ish) only after
~4,000–6,000 hours of field use. What's worth stealing is the **system**:

1. **SYL ("Speak Your Language")** as a formal contract: always attempt Spanish
   first; formally ask permission to use English; log every word you lacked; say
   new things daily.
2. **Task-based, teach-to-learn design**: language learned through the *job*
   (teaching lessons to role-played investigators from week 1), not a grammar
   sequence.
3. **The accountability cycle**: daily verbal goal-setting → plan-study-teach →
   end-of-day self-evaluation → weekly written review (Preach My Gospel ch. 7 is
   mainstream self-regulated learning codified for 19-year-olds).
4. **A separate daily drill block** (the TALL lab: SRS vocab, record-and-compare
   pronunciation) alongside — not inside — the interactive hours.
5. **Scripts as scaffolding**: memorized functional scripts that get progressively
   personalized (the church *abandoned* verbatim-memorization after a 60-year
   experiment — scripts are to be outgrown).
6. Teaching learners the **rating criteria** improved their proficiency (one BYU
   dissertation — weak evidence, cheap to adopt).

### On vocabulary

- Davies' Corpus del Español: top 1,000 lemmas ≈ 87.8% of spoken Spanish; top
  3,000 ≈ 94%. The efficient core for conversation is **full command of the top
  ~3,000 lemmas + recognition of 3,000–5,000**, plus your personal domains.
- Deliberate flashcard study is startlingly efficient (30–100 words/hour) and
  produces genuinely fluent lexical knowledge (Elgort's priming evidence), but
  should be capped near Nation's ~25% deliberate-learning strand.
- Incidental learning from input is real but slow (~10–18% uptake per encountered
  unknown word) — input builds depth/collocation, cards build breadth. Both.
- Anki + FSRS at ~10 new cards/day ≈ 15–25 min/day ≈ 120–180 hours over 18 months.
  Retention target 0.85–0.90 (workload explodes above 0.90). Don't over-optimize
  the scheduler — retrieval, delay, feedback, and sustainable load are what matter.
- Card policy: L1↔L2 pairs beat L2 definitions; production (EN→ES) cards for the
  core, recognition for breadth; one example sentence for usage; mnemonics only
  for leeches. Cloze-card orthodoxy is community folklore, not settled science.
- Cognates: drill their *pronunciation/auditory* recognition (transparency is
  orthographic, not phonological); keep a small false-friend deck.

### On fixing 15-year-old errors (the "false intermediate" risk)

- Higgs & Clifford's "terminal 2/2+" (fluency-over-accuracy learners who plateau
  permanently) was clinical observation, never a controlled study; Long (2003)
  showed *permanent* fossilization has never actually been demonstrated. Treat it
  as a **risk profile, not a verdict** — but a real one for your exact profile
  ("street-tested" travel Spanish, no correction for 15 years).
- Corrective feedback measurably remediates stabilized errors (durable d ≈ 0.6–0.8),
  with **explicit prompts >> gentle recasts**. You build a *competing correct
  routine* rather than erasing the old one — expect backsliding under cognitive
  load; durable change needs many spaced *production* retrievals of each target,
  escalating toward spontaneous use. No credible "X hours per error" number exists;
  measure per-error and reallocate.
- Spanish sequencing map: treatable now — preterite/imperfect *forms*, ser/estar
  (kill ser-overuse), clitic placement, agreement. Long-horizon, syntax-gated —
  subjunctive mood selection (Collentine: gated on reliable subordinate-clause
  production; low salience; "resists automatization"), imperfect's discourse/
  backgrounding uses, por/para nuance, null/overt subject pragmatics. Give the
  imperfect disproportionate attention; expect the subjunctive to be a multi-month
  strand, not a unit.

### On listening, reading, writing (the strands a conversation plan shortchanges)

- **Listening decoding is the binding constraint** for intermediates: micro-dictation/
  transcription training has meta-analytic support (g ≈ 0.55). The AI speaks clearly
  and adapts to you — it will never teach you to parse fast colloquial Mexican
  Spanish (resyllabification: *mis amigos* → mi.sa.mi.gos; central-Mexican vowel
  reduction: *pues* → 'ps', *para* → 'pa''). Train that separately on native audio
  (No Hay Tos → Radio Ambulante → native media), L2 captions by default (g ≈ 0.9–1.0),
  with caption-free passes to force auditory decoding. Moderate slowing (0.8x) is a
  legitimate scaffold, never the destination.
- **Extensive reading** (d ≈ 0.5–0.7 with caveats) is the cheapest engine of
  mid-frequency vocabulary depth: graded readers (B1–B2) → news → fiction at ~98%
  known-word coverage.
- **Writing + LLM correction is the de-fossilization channel.** Written corrective
  feedback works (g ≈ 0.59, retained at delay), and text preserves your exact
  errors — whereas ASR *silently normalizes* your spoken errors, making the voice
  tutor structurally blind to many of them. Run focused cycles on 1–2 treatable
  error families at a time; force staged feedback (locate → hint → self-correct →
  reveal) because LLMs default to rewriting whole sentences, which bypasses the
  learning mechanism.
- **Skill-specificity cuts both ways**: none of these substitutes for speaking
  hours, and speaking doesn't substitute for them. Declarative knowledge transfers
  across skills; procedural skill doesn't.

### On adherence (the actual #1 risk)

Base rates for 18-month self-directed programs are brutal: ~3% MOOC completion,
~0.6% completion of Rosetta Stone-length self-study, ~43% of adult app learners
inactive within 3 months. Unmodified odds of finishing are well under 20%. The
evidence-graded toolkit:

- **Strong evidence:** implementation intentions (if-then plans, d ≈ .65);
  specific *process* goals (sessions/week — outcome goals cause tunnel vision on
  complex skills); deposit commitment contracts (RCT-backed, self-selectors only);
  habit formation takes ~2–8 months for complex behaviors and **single missed days
  cost nothing**.
- **Moderate:** streaks *with repair mechanisms* (broken streaks cause
  abandonment); weekly process reports to one named person; fresh-start timing for
  restarts; temptation bundling (decays).
- **Folklore:** the 21-day habit rule; the "95% accountability" statistic
  (fabricated); public identity announcements ("I'm becoming fluent!") — evidence
  points the *other* way; visualization exercises.
- **The strongest ecological finding:** persistent adult learners *pause more but
  restart faster*. Design for lapse-recovery, not unbroken continuity. Session
  *enjoyment* predicts persistence and achievement better than motivation
  psychology — track "was this fun?" as a KPI.
- Expect a subjective plateau around months 5–9 (B1→B2 hours grow multiplicatively);
  pre-plan finer-grained metrics that keep registering progress when the level
  label stops moving.

### On assessment

- The OPI protocol (warm-up → level checks → probes to breakdown → role-play →
  wind-down; floor = sustained across all criteria, ceiling = where breakdown
  occurs) is fully published and scriptable as a simulated assessment.
- **Elicited imitation** is the best-validated quick oral proficiency proxy
  (r ≈ .66 with full measures); a validated 30-item Spanish EIT exists (Ortega
  et al. 2002). Per the critique: use it as an *anchor at hours 0 / ~250 / ~500*,
  not a monthly dipstick (it discriminates groups, not month-over-month change,
  and has ceiling compression at Advanced).
- Monthly progress instead comes from **session-derived metrics**: recorded timed
  monologues (same prompts re-recorded), errors/100 words by type, speech rate,
  filler rate, Can-Do checklist movement.
- LLMs rate *transcribed* speech and writing against CEFR descriptors at r ≈ .76
  with humans **when given descriptors + calibration exemplars**; bias direction
  varies by prompt. Never let the conversation model rate its own conversation;
  rate in a separate text session, occasionally re-submit old samples blind.
- **Voice models cannot judge pronunciation**: GPT-4o-class models perform at
  chance on pronunciation-similarity judgment. The pronunciation loop runs through
  dedicated ASR scoring (Azure Pronunciation Assessment supports **es-MX** with
  phoneme-level scores — scores as practice-targeting signals, not ground truth),
  HVPT-style perception drills, shadowing with A/B self-recording, and periodic
  human ears.
- Buy **external anchors**: an official ACTFL OPIc (purchasable through Language
  Testing International) and/or SIELE at hours ~0/250/500. This is also your
  FSI-style washback engine.
- Accent goal per FSI and Munro/Derwing: **comprehensibility, not nativeness.**
  Target the four English-speaker problem areas: tap/trill /ɾ/–/r/, approximants
  [β ð ɣ], unaspirated /p t k/, and full unreduced vowels.

### On GPT-Live-1 and the tooling reality

- GPT-Live-1 (launched 2026-07-08): full-duplex, natural interruptions, delegates
  reasoning to GPT-5.5. **No API** ("coming soon"). Plus tier: roughly **1 hr of
  GPT-Live-1 per rolling 24h** (+~2 hr of mini) — your planned session length with
  zero slack; Pro: ~12 hr. Caps change without notice; verify in-account.
- Voice chats **must start as fresh conversations** — confirming the premise that
  curriculum memory must live outside ChatGPT.
- ChatGPT **Memory is explicitly unreliable** for structured data (OpenAI says so;
  documented wipes). **Projects** (standing instructions + files + project memory)
  are the best available container, but *nothing confirms project instructions
  reliably reach voice sessions* — week-1 canary tests required.
- Documented failure modes to engineer against: **sycophancy** (under-correction
  users can't detect), **level drift** (CEFR prompting decays within even 9 turns),
  **English drift** (reproducible), **ASR-based false corrections** (the model
  corrects what it misheard, and transcripts silently normalize your errors),
  over-eager interruption of thinking pauses, weak instruction-following in voice.
- Worth stealing from commercial apps: Langua's post-session feedback report +
  conversation-mined SRS cards; Speak's per-concept mastery table driving review;
  Univerbal's post-conversation analysis; Gliglish's speed control + suggested
  replies. Notably, **none of them has a curriculum** — structure is always the
  user's job. That's what this repo is.

## 3. The honest unknowns (stated once, up front)

1. **Does AI conversation confer the same benefits as human interaction?** All
   interaction meta-analyses are human-human. An always-comprehending, infinitely
   patient interlocutor under-supplies the communication breakdowns that drive
   acquisition — and ASR blindness means it literally cannot hear some of your
   errors. Mitigations: engineered non-accommodation, breakdown simulation, human
   tutor hours as calibration. This is the plan's biggest scientific bet.
2. **Transfer to real humans** (fast, noisy, multiparty, impatient) is untested.
   FSI says multiparty conversation is the hardest skill *for their human-trained
   graduates*; it will be harder from AI-only practice. Human hours and native
   media are load-bearing, not garnish.
3. **No study validates any 500-hour curriculum**, this one included. The SLA
   evidence validates *components* (feedback, interaction, retrieval, spacing).
   Treat the plan as evidence-informed engineering with built-in measurement, and
   iterate on your own data.
4. **GPT-Live-1 is three weeks old.** Caps, Project/voice integration, Spanish
   accent quality, correction reliability — all need week-1 empirical testing and
   quarterly re-verification. Fallbacks (Gemini Live, human tutors) should be
   named in advance.
5. **Dosage for defossilizing any specific error is unknown.** Measure per-error;
   retire targets that plateau despite sustained treatment.

## 4. Resolving the target (the critic's biggest catch)

The research streams implied three incompatible targets (B2.2 "realistic" vs
"overshoot ILR 3" vs "B2 took missionaries 4,000–6,000 hours"). The resolution:

- **Committed 500-hour target: ACTFL Advanced Mid speaking (≈ B2+), verified by
  official OPIc.** Narrate/describe across time frames in paragraph-length
  discourse, handle complications, be understood by natives unaccustomed to
  learners — comfortably, on wide-ranging topics.
- **Stretch probes, not commitments:** Superior functions (supported opinion,
  hypothesis, abstraction) trained in the final 100 hours.
- Why this is credible against the discouraging comparisons: FSI's ~1,000 total
  hours is *from zero* to ILR 3 (≈ Superior — a level above this target); the
  missionary 4,000–6,000-hour figure is uninstructed field use by learners who
  started from nothing and never did accuracy work; you start from dormant B1+
  with savings effects, and every hour here is engineered.
- **Endgame per FSI's critical-mass finding:** at Advanced Mid you are below the
  self-maintaining threshold, so the plan must end with a standing maintenance
  design (weekly conversation + ongoing input), or continue past 500 hours toward
  Superior. Decide at hour 400, with data.

## 5. Proposed hour accounting (the 500 hours, defined)

"500 hours" = total study time, all strands. Modified four-strands allocation for
a conversation-primary goal:

| Strand | Hours | % | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live AI conversation sessions | ~180 | 36% | The daily ~1-hr GPT-Live-1 session (meaning-focused output + interaction + in-session fluency work) |
| Listening | ~110 | 22% | Decoding micro-dictations, captioned video, podcast ladder, shadowing |
| Reading | ~55 | 11% | Graded readers → news → fiction |
| Deliberate study | ~90 | 18% | Anki (~60h), grammar focus-on-form cycles, pronunciation drills |
| Writing + feedback | ~40 | 8% | Daily short writing with staged LLM correction (the defossilization channel) |
| Assessment & review | ~25 | 5% | Weekly reviews, monthly benchmarks, EIT/OPIc anchors, error-ledger upkeep |

Cadence: ~7–9 hr/week ≈ 14–16 months. Conversation sessions ≥3×/week (short-lag
proceduralization); vocab on long FSRS spacing; themes recycled at multi-week
gaps. Density floor ~5 hr/week — below that, gains get inconsistent; if life
compresses a week, shorten sessions before cutting frequency. 1–2 planned
"deload" weeks per quarter, framed as checkpoints (not fresh starts).

Phases (sketch — the full plan comes later):

- **P0 (wk 1): Baseline.** EIT, simulated OPI, Can-Do inventory, recorded
  monologues, vocab diagnostic over top-5k lemmas, error census → seed the ledger.
  Plus GPT-Live-1 canary tests (§6.7).
- **P1 (hrs 1–75): Reactivation.** Forced-production retrieval of dormant
  language; error census hardens into target list; dormant-word Anki blitz;
  functional script set built and personalized.
- **P2 (hrs 75–250): Systematic rebuild.** Grammar campaign in focused cycles
  (imperfect front-loaded, ser/estar, clitics, agreement); task-based sessions;
  decoding training ramps; subjunctive strand opens once subordinate clauses are
  reliable. Expect the plateau near the end of this phase — metrics switch
  emphasis to fine-grained gains.
- **P3 (hrs 250–400): Expansion.** Domain rotation (work, politics, humor, service
  encounters — the anti-"mission Spanish" move); multiparty simulation; native-
  rate media; monthly Spanish-only immersion days (DLI-style), escalating.
- **P4 (hrs 400–500+): Polish & push.** Superior-function probes, accent
  comprehensibility work, final OPIc, maintenance design.

## 6. System architecture (the "AI memory" problem)

### 6.1 Principle

**ChatGPT is a stateless compute resource; this repo is the brain.** Every voice
session starts fresh by design. All curriculum state lives in files you own; you
(plus text-mode ChatGPT) are the transport layer between sessions.

### 6.2 The state files (this repo, eventually)

```
state/
  learner-profile.md      # level estimates, goals, dialect target, preferences
  curriculum-state.md     # current phase, week, active targets, next 3 sessions
  error-ledger.md         # per-error: feature tag, acquisition class
                          #  (treatable / late-variable / interface-persistent),
                          #  prompted vs spontaneous accuracy over time, status
  concept-mastery.md      # per grammar concept: mastery score, last-seen, next-due
  vocab/                  # Davies top-5k tracking: known / dormant / unknown / due
  personas.md             # recurring AI characters + their state (MTC "investigator" analog)
  scripts.md              # functional scripts in rotation + personalization stage
  session-logs/           # one structured debrief per session (append-only)
  assessments/            # recordings, transcripts, scores, EIT results, OPIc reports
  adherence.md            # cumulative hours (headline), weekly-target streak +
                          #  repair tokens, if-then plans, lapse restart script
```

### 6.3 The session loop (the core ritual)

1. **Pre-session (5 min, scripted):** generate today's *session header* from the
   state files — a compact block: level anchor with CEFR descriptor examples,
   correction protocol, SYL contract, today's task, 5–10 target items (due vocab +
   2–3 active error targets), persona state. Paste/read it into the fresh session.
2. **The hour (structured, 3–4 blocks — each transition re-anchors the protocol,
   which counters level drift):**
   - *Warm-up + retrieval quiz* (~5–10 min): model cold-quizzes last session's
     mined items (testing effect; the header is the spaced schedule).
   - *Task block* (~25 min): a communicative task with an outcome — teach-a-lesson,
     roleplay-with-complication, negotiation, interview. Not open-ended chat.
   - *Fluency block* (~10 min): 4/3/2 repeated monologue on one topic, feedback
     between retellings.
   - *Focus-on-form block* (~10 min): communicative drills on 1–2 ledger targets;
     model prompts self-repair, never just supplies the form.
   - *Wrap (minute ~50 — sessions die at the cap):* spoken self-evaluation against
     the day's goals (MTC cycle), then end.
3. **Post-session (10–15 min, text mode on the transcript):** structured debrief
   prompt → fixed-schema output (errors heard + which were self-corrected, new/
   failed vocab, target-item performance, task outcome, next-session focus,
   fun-rating). Append to `session-logs/`; update ledger/mastery/Anki. The
   *transcript*, not the model's live impressions, is ground truth — and even it
   can normalize your errors, so spot-check audio for suspicious "corrections."

### 6.4 Correction protocol (resolving the recast-vs-prompt contradiction)

- **Ledger targets (things you partly know — the dominant case):** prompts first
  ("¿Estás seguro del tiempo verbal?"), elicitation, clarification requests —
  force self-repair, then confirm. This is the d≈0.83 move, and it counters
  audio-recast invisibility.
- **New/unknown forms:** brief explicit recast, flagged saliently ("Ojo: se dice
  X"), logged for later.
- **Flow-critical moments (mid-task):** let it go, log it; the end-of-session
  review catches it (immediate-vs-delayed evidence suggests little is lost, and
  the review doubles as retrieval).
- **Anti-sycophancy clauses in every header:** minimum correction quota, no
  unearned praise, "your job is to find errors, not to be liked," periodic
  planted-error checks (§6.7) to verify it's actually correcting.

### 6.5 Assessment calendar

| Cadence | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Every session | Debrief metrics: errors/100 words by type, target-item hit rate, self-eval |
| Weekly | Written review (MTC-style): hours, streak state, what's working, plan deltas |
| Monthly | Recorded timed monologues (fixed prompt set), rated later in a separate text session against pasted ACTFL/CEFR descriptors + calibration exemplars; writing sample; Can-Do checklist pass |
| Quarterly | Simulated OPI (scripted 4-phase protocol); pronunciation benchmark via Azure PA (es-MX); blind re-rating of one old sample (drift check) |
| Hours ~0 / 250 / 500 | Spanish EIT (parallel forms) + **official OPIc via LTI** (the paid, human-rated ground truth and washback engine) |

### 6.6 The pronunciation loop (separate from the voice tutor)

Voice model's role: comprehensibility-level feedback and phonetics *explanations*
only. The actual loop: HVPT-style multi-talker minimal-pair perception drills on
the four problem areas → explicit articulatory mini-lessons → shadowing native
audio (No Hay Tos-grade) with A/B self-recording → Azure Pronunciation Assessment
es-MX for phoneme-level practice targeting → occasional human verification
(tutor or Speechling). Goal: comprehensibility metrics, not accent elimination.

### 6.7 Week-1 canary tests for GPT-Live-1 (before trusting anything)

1. Plant a fact in Project files → start a voice session inside the Project → ask
   for it. (Do Project instructions/files reach voice at all?)
2. Deliberately make 5 planted grammar errors → count how many get flagged under
   the correction protocol. Repeat at minutes 5 and 45 (drift measurement).
3. Deliberately mispronounce minimal pairs (pero/perro, caso/queso) → does it
   notice? (Expected: no — confirms routing pronunciation elsewhere.)
4. Measure: time-to-first-English-drift under a Spanish-only header; whether
   "en español, por favor" recovers it; interruption behavior during long pauses.
5. Verify actual voice caps in-account; test session-death-at-60-min behavior.
6. Ask it to sustain B1-anchored output for 15 min → have text-mode rate the
   transcript's level stability afterward.

### 6.8 Adherence architecture (from the folklore-audited toolkit)

- **Headline metric: cumulative hours toward 500** (unbreakable). Secondary:
  weekly-target streak *with pre-funded repair tokens*. No daily streaks.
- **If-then plans in `adherence.md`**: default slot + explicit fallback ("if I
  miss the morning, a 20-min micro-session after dinner counts").
- **Pre-written lapse restart script**: shortened comeback sessions, zero make-up
  debt, restart on a Monday/1st (fresh-start timing). Lapses are expected events;
  fast re-engagement is the success pattern.
- **Weekly process report** (hours, sessions — not identity claims) to one named
  person or channel. Optional deposit contract if that's your temperament.
- **Fun is a KPI**: session topics you actually care about; kill activity formats
  that consistently rate un-fun — enjoyment predicts persistence better than any
  motivation exercise.
- **Month 5–9 plateau plan**: pre-scheduled program refresh + metric-granularity
  switch, framed as a checkpoint.
- Process goals are the commitments; CEFR levels are measurements, not weekly
  goals.

### 6.9 Division of labor

| Job | Tool |
|---|---|
| Conversation, roleplay, interaction, recasts/prompts, fluency blocks | GPT-Live-1 voice (inside the Project) |
| Transcript mining, structured debriefs, grammar explanations, WCF on writing, monologue rating, quiz generation | ChatGPT text mode (same Project) |
| All SRS scheduling | Anki + FSRS (learner-owned) |
| Pronunciation scoring | Azure PA (es-MX) + self-recording A/B + periodic humans |
| Listening decoding | Native podcasts/TV + transcripts (micro-dictation) |
| Ground-truth proficiency | LTI OPIc / SIELE (paid, ~3×) |
| Human calibration + multiparty | italki tutors / exchange partners / Mexico trips (~10–15% of speaking hours) |
| Curriculum state, memory, analytics | This repo (+ scripts later, if wanted) |

  slack) or Pro if double sessions matter. 
- OPIc ≈ $75–100 × 3; italki ≈ $10–25/hr × 30–50 hrs; Azure PA free tier likely
  sufficient; Anki free; graded readers ~$50.
- Realistic total: **~$800–1,800 over 16 months on Plus**; Pro-tier raises it to

- Contingencies: caps change without notice (fallback: GPT-Live-1 mini for

  if the GPT-Live API ships mid-program, the architecture upgrades naturally
  (automated logging, real system prompts) without changing the file schema.
- Privacy note: hundreds of hours of your voice will transit OpenAI/Microsoft;
  keep recordings/transcripts you care about in your own storage.

## 8. What this plan deliberately rejects

- **Gamification-first design** (Duolingo): streak-maximization and XP are
  engagement mechanics for the app's retention, not your acquisition. (We keep
