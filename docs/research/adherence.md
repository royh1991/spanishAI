# adherence

## Summary
Attrition — not method quality — is the dominant failure mode for an 18-month self-directed language plan, and the base rates are brutal. MOOC completion runs ~3% overall (and roughly half even among paying verified learners), Rosetta Stone-style self-study attrition approaches 100% (only 21% of enrollees logged even 10 of 200 hours; 0.6% finished), a large 14-year survival analysis of adult app learners found ~43% stop active engagement within the first three months, and even paid research participants drop out at 26-36% over just 8 weeks. The hazard is heavily front-loaded: app churn concentrates in days 3-7 and month 1-3; New Year's resolution data show ~23% quit within a week and ~45% within a month, with ~40-46% surviving to six months. A realistic planning assumption is that unmodified base-rate odds of still studying at month 12-18 are well under 20%, so the plan's architecture should be judged primarily on how it manages the month-1-to-3 cliff and the month-6+ plateau, not on pedagogical optimality.

The motivation literature offers useful but honest-sized tools. The best-evidenced mechanisms are: implementation intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta-analysis, d=.65 across 94 tests — the single strongest lab-validated lever, though effects shrink at scale); habit formation with context-stable cues (Lally 2010 and a 2024 systematic review: median ~59-66 days to automaticity, up to ~11 months for complex behaviors — and missing a single day does not derail formation); and deposit-style commitment contracts (Giné/Karlan/Zinman RCT: real, durable effects, but only 11% take-up and most takers forfeit their money — they work best for people who self-select in). Weaker or double-edged: temptation bundling (real but decaying effects that collapse after schedule disruptions), fresh-start effects (good for re-starts after lapses, but anticipated landmarks license present slacking), public goal announcement (identity-level announcements reduced follow-through in Gollwitzer's 2009 studies, while weekly progress reports to a peer helped in the small, non-peer-reviewed Matthews study — share process updates, not identity claims), and streaks (intact streaks genuinely boost engagement, but broken streaks cause disproportionate abandonment unless a repair mechanism exists). The famous "accountability makes you 95% likely to succeed" ASTD statistic is fabricated folklore.

On what predicts adherence in employed adults: Dörnyei's ideal L2 self strongly predicts intended effort (r≈.61) but only weakly predicts actual achievement (r≈.20); the L2 learning experience — whether the sessions themselves are enjoyable — is the more consistent predictor of both, which argues for optimizing session enjoyment over vision exercises. Grit meta-analytically adds little beyond conscientiousness (r≈.15-.17 with performance), with the perseverance-of-effort facet doing all the work. Directed motivational currents exist but are surge phenomena lasting weeks-to-months and cannot be engineered on demand for 18 months. The B2/intermediate plateau is real as an experienced phenomenon (Richards 2008; corpus evidence shows minimal error-rate differences between B2 and C1) but is substantially a measurement artifact: per-level hour requirements grow roughly multiplicatively, so subjective progress stalls even when learning continues — meaning the plan needs progress metrics that keep resolving small gains after month 6. Scaled re-engagement interventions (Duolingo's bandit-optimized notifications: +2% retention; MOOC planning prompts: early boost that fades; Kizilcec et al. PNAS 2020: no intervention generalized) all show small effects, so the honest conclusion is that lapse-tolerant design — treating disengagement/dormancy/re-engagement cycles as normal and pre-planning the restart — beats any single motivational gadget.

## Findings
### Base rate: MOOC completion is ~3% overall and did not improve over six years; roughly half of registrants never start
Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente (Science, 2019, 'The MOOC Pivot') analyzed 565 HarvardX/MITx courses, 12.7M registrations over 6.5 years: 3.13% completed in 2017-18 (down from ~6% in 2014-15); 52% never started; completion fell even among learners who stated intent to complete and among paying verified-track learners (who complete at roughly 45-50%). Payment/intent helps a lot but still leaves ~half of committed payers not finishing a single course far shorter than 18 months.
Sources: https://www.insidehighered.com/digital-learning/article/2019/01/16/study-offers-data-show-moocs-didnt-achieve-their-goals; https://openpraxis.org/articles/10.55982/openpraxis.16.3.606

### Base rate: self-study language software attrition approaches 100%; even paid study participants drop 26-36% in 8 weeks
Nielsen (2011) volunteer data: only 21% of Rosetta Stone users exceeded 10 hours of a 200-hour course; 1 of 150 (0.6%) completed final assessment; Tell Me More: 2.2% finished. Vesselinov & Grego's 8-week Duolingo efficacy study lost ~36% of 1,193 participants, and finishers averaged 34.4 of the requested 50 hours; their Babbel study and a 2023 Babbel/Duolingo comparison saw ~20-26% attrition in 8 weeks despite researcher contact. Classroom analogs are similar (82% attrition across 4 US high-school levels; only 17% of college language enrollees reach upper division).
Sources: http://backseatlinguist.com/blog/and-then-there-were-none-surviving-foreign-language-study/; https://theowlapp.health/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/DuolingoReport_Final-1.pdf; https://assets.ctfassets.net/zuzqvf4m2o58/5eYRgCslJnJBF9yhZKgX01/78b93f75ca40fca6c7b927b6e2e82bf8/Babbel-Efficacy-Study.pdf; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09588221.2023.2215294

### Dropout timing is heavily front-loaded: days 3-7, then months 1-3, are the danger zones
Duolingo sees ~50% churn by day 7 with sharp decline after day 3; monthly churn was 47% in 2020, ~28% in Western markets by late 2023. A 14-year recurrent-event survival analysis of 3,670 adult library-based Mango Languages users (Studies in Second Language Acquisition) found ~43% discontinued active engagement within the first 3 months — the highest-risk period for every learner profile; a related MALL clustering study found 29-37% dropped after their first active month. MOOCs lose ~half of starters in week 1 (flexible-schedule courses hit 50% week-1 abandonment vs 17% for rigid schedules). Norcross's resolution research: ~23% quit within 1 week, ~45% within a month, 40-46% still successful at 6 months, ~19% at 2 years. Nothing in the literature suggests a large second cliff at a specific later month, but plateau-linked demotivation accumulates from ~6 months on.
Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/acceptance-and-engagement-patterns-of-mobileassisted-language-learning-among-nonconventional-adult-l2-learners-a-survival-analysis/C1186B329F808A11DDF2C748E77FD1EE; https://sensortower.com/blog/duolingo-redefining-engagement-in-the-ed-tech-space; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jclp.1151; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-22244-4_20

### Persistence in adult self-directed learners is cyclical, not continuous — design for dormancy and re-engagement, not unbroken streaks
The Mango survival analysis reconceptualizes adult MALL persistence as 'cyclical phases of engagement, disengagement, dormancy, and reengagement.' Counterintuitively, the most persistent adult learners paused MORE often but re-engaged faster and accumulated more total engagement (45-47% lower dropout risk). This is the strongest ecological evidence available for an adult app-based learner over long horizons: the winning pattern is fast lapse recovery, not zero lapses.
Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/acceptance-and-engagement-patterns-of-mobileassisted-language-learning-among-nonconventional-adult-l2-learners-a-survival-analysis/C1186B329F808A11DDF2C748E77FD1EE

### L2 motivation theory: ideal L2 self drives intended effort but barely predicts achievement; session enjoyment (L2 learning experience) is the workhorse variable
Meta-analyses of Dörnyei's L2 Motivational Self System (39 samples, 32,078 learners) find ideal L2 self correlates r=.61 with intended effort but only r≈.20-.26 with objective achievement; the L2 learning experience correlates r≈.27-.31 with achievement — the most consistent predictor — with substantial heterogeneity. Directed Motivational Currents (Dörnyei, Ibrahim & Muir) are validated as a real phenomenon (intense goal-directed surges) mainly via qualitative and survey studies, but they are episodic (weeks-to-months), cannot be reliably induced, and are not a plausible 18-month engine. Grit: Credé et al.'s meta-analysis (66,807 people) shows grit predicts performance weakly (r=.15-.17), overlaps heavily with conscientiousness, and only the perseverance-of-effort facet has predictive value; domain-specific L2 grit does somewhat better and a 2025 study found L2 grit and age predict MALL attrition. Practical translation: making the daily session intrinsically pleasant has better evidential standing than motivation-visualization exercises, and 'consistency of interest' questionnaires add nothing actionable.
Sources: https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ssllt/article/view/12295; https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16060892; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305228331_The_dynamics_of_intense_long-term_motivation_in_language_learning_Directed_Motivational_Currents_in_theory_and_practice; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10506257/; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025000809

### Implementation intentions are the best lab-validated adherence mechanism (d=.65), but effects shrink when scaled
Gollwitzer & Sheeran's meta-analysis (94 independent tests) found if-then planning ('If it is 7am Tuesday, then I do my Spanish session at my desk') improves goal attainment with d=.65 — medium-to-large, one of the most replicated effects in self-regulation. Caveat: when Kizilcec, Reich, Yeomans et al. tested plan-making prompts at scale (250,000 learners, 250 MOOCs; PNAS 2020), they produced only short-lived early-engagement boosts that faded over the course, no intervention generalized across contexts, and ML could not predict who would benefit. Value-affirmation writing actually hurt learners in developed countries. Conclusion: use if-then plans (cheap, positive expected value) but do not expect them to carry 18 months alone; effects need periodic renewal.
Sources: https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes; https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2020/06/study-no-single-solution-helps-all-students-complete-moocs

### Habit formation: realistic time-to-automaticity is ~2 months minimum, up to ~11 months for complex behaviors; single missed days are harmless; rigid time-of-day routinization is NOT clearly supported
Lally et al. 2010 (EJSP): median 66 days to automaticity plateau, range 18-254; more complex behaviors take longer; missing one day did not materially impair habit formation. Singh et al. 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis (20 studies, 2,601 participants): habits begin forming at ~59-66 days median but can take up to 335 days; morning-performed behaviors, planning, and enjoyment were determinants of faster formation. On context-stable cues the evidence genuinely conflicts: correlational studies (Schumacher 2019 weight-loss maintainers; a longitudinal Calm-app study) find temporally consistent practicers adhere better, but the Beshears/Milkman RCT (Management Science 2021) found paying people to exercise in a fixed daily 2-hour window produced FEWER visits during and after incentives than flexible-timing incentives. For a working adult, a default time slot plus explicit permission to move the session beats a rigid slot. A one-hour conversation session is a 'complex behavior' — budget 4-8 months of scaffolded effort before it feels automatic, not 21 or 66 days.
Sources: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674; https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/oby.22535; https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3706; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10131734/

### Commitment devices: real, durable effects in RCTs, but low take-up and most participants forfeit their stakes — they are for self-selectors and carry a demoralization risk
Giné, Karlan & Zinman's CARES RCT (deposit contract for smoking, AEJ:Applied 2010): intention-to-treat effect of ~3-6 percentage points on biochemically verified quitting at 6 months, persisting at surprise 12-month tests — but only 11% of offered smokers took it up, and roughly two-thirds of takers lost their money. stickK (built by Karlan/Ayres) and Beeminder implement this mechanism but have published no RCTs of their own platforms; their public success statistics are marketing, not evidence. Prepaying tutors/classes is a soft commitment device with only indirect evidence (sunk-cost-driven attendance). Verdict: deposit contracts are among the few mechanisms with causal evidence of 12-month behavior change, best deployed voluntarily, with stakes sized to sting but not to make a forfeiture feel like program failure.
Sources: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.2.4.213; https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/put_your_money_where_your_butt_is.pdf

### Public goal announcement can backfire; progress-report accountability probably helps; the famous 95% accountability statistic is fabricated
Gollwitzer et al. 2009 (Psychological Science, 4 studies): when identity-relevant intentions ('I will become a Spanish speaker') were noticed by others, follow-through DECREASED — social acknowledgment creates a premature sense of identity completeness; effect held for strongly committed people. Caveats: small pre-replication-crisis lab samples; applies to identity announcements, not progress reporting. Matthews' Dominican study (n=267): those who wrote goals, made action commitments, and sent weekly progress reports to a friend reported ~76% success vs ~43% for think-only controls — but it is small, self-reported, self-selected, and never peer-reviewed. The '65% with accountability partner / 95% with appointments' ASTD figure cited across the coaching industry has no published methodology or dataset and appears to be an organizational myth like the fake 1953 Yale goals study. In the 250k-learner MOOC PNAS study, a social-accountability prompt gave only a fading early boost. Net: report weekly process metrics to a specific person; do not broadcast identity aspirations.
Sources: https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/c/6235/files/2019/02/gollwitzer-et-al-2009-when-intentions-go-public.pdf; https://scholar.dominican.edu/psychology-faculty-conference-presentations/3/; https://www.accountablo.com/blog/ai-accountability-partner; https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2020/06/study-no-single-solution-helps-all-students-complete-moocs

### Temptation bundling and fresh starts: modest, decaying, situation-specific effects
Milkman, Minson & Volpp (Management Science 2014, n=226): gym-locked audiobooks raised attendance 51% vs control initially, but effects decayed across the 9-week study and collapsed after the Thanksgiving break — schedule disruptions kill bundles; 61% later paid for the restriction, showing demand. Fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis, Management Science 2014): goal initiation reliably spikes after temporal landmarks (new week/month/year, birthdays) in archival data — useful for scheduling restarts after lapses and launching new program phases. Dark side: Koo, Dai, Mai & Song (OBHDP 2020) show ANTICIPATED landmarks license present-day slacking on ongoing goals ('I'll get serious in January'), so mid-program landmarks should be framed as checkpoints, not new beginnings.
Sources: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256040779_Holding_the_Hunger_Games_Hostage_at_the_Gym_An_Evaluation_of_Temptation_Bundling; https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2204126; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749597820303642

### Streaks work while intact but create abandonment risk when broken; repair mechanisms neutralize the downside
Silverman & Barasch (Journal of Consumer Research 2022/2023, 7 studies): highlighting an intact logged streak increases subsequent engagement (the streak itself becomes a goal), but highlighting a broken streak reduces engagement relative to intact — worst when the break is self-attributed — and the negative effect is attenuated when the streak can be 'repaired.' Duolingo's operational data is consistent: streaks plus repair mechanics (streak freezes) support retention (10M+ users with 365-day streaks), and its bandit-optimized reminder notifications yielded a causal but small +2% new-user retention (Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020). Design translation for a file-based tracker: track a streak of 'weeks with target met' rather than consecutive days, build in explicit freeze/repair tokens, and log cumulative hours (which can never be 'broken') as the headline metric.
Sources: https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/49/6/1095/6623414; https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf; https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification

### Goals: specific and difficult beats vague; for complex skill acquisition, process/learning goals beat outcome goals
Locke & Latham's goal-setting program (35+ years, hundreds of studies) robustly shows specific difficult goals outperform 'do your best.' Critically for language learning: on new complex tasks, outcome goals cause 'tunnel vision' and can impair performance; assigned learning goals (acquire strategies/knowledge) do better. The 2022 sport meta-analysis found goal setting works overall but direct process-vs-outcome comparisons are surprisingly thin (only two mixed-result studies combining them) — so 'process goals beat outcome goals' is well-grounded theoretically and for complex tasks specifically, but is not a slam-dunk universal finding. Self-regulated-learning intervention meta-analyses in online environments show reliable but small-to-moderate effects (overall SRL-achievement correlation r≈.14; time management, effort regulation, and metacognitive monitoring are the strategies that matter; an L2-specific meta-analysis finds moderate effects on achievement and self-efficacy).
Sources: https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/locke%20latham%20new%20dir%20gs%20curr%20dir%20psy%20sci%202006.pdf; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2022.2116723; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9650592/; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389285302_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Correlation_Between_Self-Regulated_Learning_Strategies_and_Academic_Performance_in_Online_and_Blended_Learning_Environments

### The intermediate/B2 plateau is real as an experience but substantially a measurement artifact; its motivational danger exceeds its linguistic reality
Richards (2008, Cambridge) canonized the plateau: intermediate learners develop a functional-but-fossilized version of the language (fluent, communicative, limited range/precision) and progress feels flat between B1 and B2/C1; one corpus study found no significant error-frequency difference between B2 and C1 writing in most categories. But Richards' account is pedagogical synthesis, not strong longitudinal evidence. Structural causes are well understood: (a) vocabulary follows Zipf's law, so each new band of mid-frequency words yields less visible communicative payoff; (b) hours-per-CEFR-level grow roughly multiplicatively (FSI/Cambridge estimates), so equal effort produces less measured movement; (c) comprehension needs stop forcing improvement once the learner can 'get by' — the false-intermediate's exact trap. Practical upshot: expect a subjective stall around months 5-9 for a rusty AP-4 learner crossing B1→B2, and pre-plan finer-grained progress metrics (can-do statements, error-type counts, speech-rate/complexity measures from session transcripts) that continue registering gains when global level labels stop moving.
Sources: https://www.professorjackrichards.com/wp-content/uploads/moving-beyond-the-plateau.pdf; https://tpls.academypublication.com/index.php/tpls/article/download/8452/6861/26095; https://tortolingua.com/blog/language-learning-plateau/

### Sustainable load: ~5+ hours/week appears to be the effective floor; concentrated study outperforms thin spreading; elite-practice research caps focused daily practice around 1-4 hours with deliberate rest
Serrano & Muñoz compared identical 110-hour EFL programs delivered extensively (~2 hrs/week over 7 months), semi-intensively, and intensively (20 hrs/week over 5 weeks): more concentrated formats produced better outcomes — a consistent finding across a dozen-plus intensity studies and an apparent paradox versus the item-level spacing effect (item-level SRS spacing remains valid within a concentrated program). Adult-learner program data suggest <4-5 hrs/week produces inconsistent gains. Ericsson's deliberate-practice research (1993) found elite performers sustain ~3.5-4 hrs/day maximum in 60-90-minute blocks with naps and ~8 hours sleep, and 'the effective duration of deliberate practice may be closer to 1 hr per day' for less-supported practicers — so a working adult's 1 hr/day (~7 hrs/week) is near the sweet spot: above the efficacy floor, below cognitive-fatigue territory. Honest caveat: there is no direct RCT on 'optimal weekly hours for employed adult language learners over 18 months'; deliberate rest / periodization advice (e.g., planned lighter weeks) is extrapolated from exercise science and expertise research, not language-specific trials.
Sources: https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2023/02/07/learning-fast-or-slow/; https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/macnamara-maitra-2019-the-role-of-deliberate-practice-in-expert-performance-revisiting-ericsson-krampe-tesch-romer.pdf; https://bulletproofmusician.com/how-many-hours-a-day-should-you-practice/

### Re-engagement interventions have small, fading effects at scale; the strongest documented pattern is that resolvers who treat lapses as information, not failure, persist
Evidence inventory: Duolingo's contextual-bandit notification system (200M notifications) bought +2% new-user retention — the best-documented scaled re-engagement machine, and its yield is 2%. MOOC plan-making/accountability prompts gave early boosts that faded (PNAS 2020). Norcross's resolution studies found most successful resolvers lapsed at least once — successful maintainers differed in responding to slips with renewed effort rather than abandonment (consistent with the abstinence-violation-effect literature in addiction relapse). The Mango survival analysis shows resilient adults cycle through dormancy and return quickly. No published intervention reliably resurrects a fully lapsed self-directed language learner; the leverage is in pre-committed lapse protocols (if-then relapse plans, fresh-start-timed restarts, repairable streaks) written before the lapse happens.
Sources: https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf; https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2020/06/study-no-single-solution-helps-all-students-complete-moocs; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jclp.1151; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/acceptance-and-engagement-patterns-of-mobileassisted-language-learning-among-nonconventional-adult-l2-learners-a-survival-analysis/C1186B329F808A11DDF2C748E77FD1EE

### Folklore audit: which popular mechanisms have real evidence
STRONG: implementation intentions (meta-analysis, d=.65); specific difficult goals and learning/process goals for complex tasks (Locke & Latham); deposit commitment contracts (RCT, 12-month persistence, with take-up/forfeiture caveats); habit formation over ~2-11 months with tolerance for missed days (Lally; Singh 2024). MODERATE: streaks-with-repair (JCR experiments + Duolingo operational data); temptation bundling (RCT, decaying); fresh-start timing for restarts (archival); weekly progress-report accountability (one small non-peer-reviewed study plus scaled prompt evidence that fades); temporal consistency of practice time (correlational only, and one RCT found rigid routinization backfired). WEAK/FOLKLORE: 21-day (or 66-day-as-guarantee) habit rules; the ASTD 65%/95% accountability statistics (fabricated); public announcement of goals as a motivator (evidence points the other way for identity goals); grit as a trainable superpower (weak incremental validity); directed motivational currents as a plannable mechanism; 'visualize your ideal self' as an achievement driver (predicts intentions, barely predicts outcomes).
Sources: https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes; https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488; https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.2.4.213; https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/49/6/1095/6623414; https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3706

## Design implications
- Plan for the base rate, not the aspiration: build the program assuming a >50% chance of at least one multi-week lapse and make month 1-3 deliberately easy to sustain (the survival hazard peaks there). Front-load novelty and quick wins; delay ambitious weekly loads until the habit is 3+ months old.
- Make the headline metric cumulative and unbreakable (total hours toward 500, sessions completed), track a repairable weekly-target streak rather than a daily streak, and pre-fund 'streak freeze' tokens in the tracking files so a missed day or travel week is an accounted-for event, not a broken identity.
- Write if-then implementation intentions into the curriculum state files: a default session slot (evidence favors mornings for automaticity) PLUS explicit fallback rules ('if I miss the morning slot, then 20-minute micro-session after dinner counts') — the Beshears RCT warns against rigid single-window scheduling for working adults.
- Engineer a lapse-recovery protocol BEFORE it is needed: a written 'restart script' (shortened comeback sessions, no make-up debt, scheduled on a fresh-start landmark like Monday or the 1st), because fast re-engagement after dormancy — not unbroken continuity — is what distinguishes persistent adult learners.
- Optimize the sessions themselves for enjoyment (topics the learner actually likes, conversational wins, humor) — L2 learning experience predicts both effort and achievement better than ideal-self visualization; treat 'was this session fun?' as a tracked KPI alongside linguistic metrics.
- Use accountability as weekly process reports (hours done, sessions done) to one named person or channel; avoid public identity announcements ('I'm becoming fluent'). If using money, a voluntary deposit contract (stickK/Beeminder-style) has the best causal evidence, sized so forfeiture stings but doesn't end the program.
- Pre-plan the B1→B2 plateau (likely months 5-9 for this learner): switch progress measurement to fine-grained, session-derived metrics (error counts by type, speech rate, filler-word rate, can-do checklist items) that keep registering gains when global level stops moving; schedule a deliberate program redesign/'new phase' at that point to exploit fresh-start framing.
- Target ~5-8 hours/week (the ~1 hr/day design is well within the effective floor of ~5 hrs/week and the deliberate-practice ceiling); prefer keeping intensity up in concentrated blocks over thinning to 2 hrs/week during busy periods — if load must drop, shorten the sessions before cutting frequency, and schedule 1-2 planned lighter 'deload' weeks per quarter (extrapolated, not language-RCT-proven).
- Set process goals (sessions/week, hours/month) as the accountable commitments and treat outcome goals (CEFR level, test scores) as periodic measurements only — outcome-goal tunnel vision on a complex skill is counterproductive, and per-level hour requirements guarantee outcome milestones will arrive slower over time.
- Expect the conversation-session habit to take 4-8 months to feel automatic (complex behavior, upper range of Lally/Singh distributions); do not interpret month-2 effortfulness as a motivation defect, and do not let a single missed day carry any narrative weight — the habit-formation data show it costs nothing.

## Open questions
- No study directly measures 12/18-month persistence for serious self-directed learners using AI conversation partners — all base rates are proxies (apps, MOOCs, resolutions); whether an engaging real-time voice tutor changes the survival curve is unknown and could plausibly cut either way (higher session enjoyment vs. no human social obligation).
- The intensity-vs-spacing paradox (Serrano & Muñoz) has not been tested at the 1 hr/day × 18 months design point, and long-term retention comparisons (years later) between intensive and distributed formats are missing.
- Gollwitzer's public-announcement backfire is pre-replication-crisis lab work with small samples and no direct replication located; the practical rule (share process, not identity) is a reasonable synthesis but rests on thin ice in both directions (the Matthews accountability study is equally weak).
- Deposit-contract evidence comes from smoking/exercise; no RCT tests financial stakes on language-study adherence specifically, and the demoralization cost of forfeiting a stake mid-program (does it end the whole endeavor?) is unstudied.
- Whether the B2 plateau reflects genuine acquisition slowdown versus measurement granularity remains empirically unresolved — longitudinal corpus studies tracking the same adult learners through B1→C1 with fine-grained metrics are scarce.
- The 2025 finding that L2 grit and age predict MALL attrition (Learning and Individual Differences) could not be accessed in full (paywall); its effect sizes and which grit facet drives attrition are worth retrieving.
- Optimal deload/rest periodization for cognitive skill learning is extrapolated from exercise science and Ericsson's expertise work; no language-learning trial tests planned rest weeks against continuous study.
- Re-engagement of fully lapsed learners is an evidence desert: known interventions (notifications, planning prompts) yield ~2% or fading effects, and no study tests pre-committed personal relapse protocols for study habits, though addiction-relapse theory supports them.
